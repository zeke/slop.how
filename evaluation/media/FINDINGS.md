# First media pilot: continue evaluation, hold the API

2026-10-13. 77 successful calls, no failures, $0.016719096 estimated inference
cost. No new media was generated by AI. 29 media representations: 24 images and
five video inputs, with related variants grouped together. All are development
samples; the 22-image base corpus spans eight source groups. Labels are
assistant judgments recorded before inference, not human-reviewed ground truth.

## What the first batch says

The material-sheen question is too broad for launch. It confuses intentional
rendering and polished surfaces with a defect, even with explicit exclusions.
Clef flagged the photographed real avocado chair at 0.5391. Flash and omni
flagged the intentionally cartoon-like emoji chair at 0.7976 and 0.7342.
These are concrete rubric failures worth fixing before expanding the API.

At the exploratory threshold of 0.5, disagreement counts against provisional
negative labels were:

| Factor          | Clef | Flash | Omni |
| --------------- | ---- | ----- | ---- |
| Garbled text    | 0/20 | 0/20  | 0/20 |
| Plastic sheen   | 8/19 | 7/19  | 2/19 |
| Anatomy errors  | 0/21 | 0/21  | 0/21 |
| Stock cliché    | 0/20 | 4/20  | 1/20 |

These denominators count images, not independent source groups. They exclude
unassessable labels. They are development-set diagnostics, not estimates of
production accuracy. The base corpus contains only one provisional positive
for sheen and one for stock composition (the same generated chair). All three
flagged its sheen; clef and flash flagged its stock composition, omni did not.
There are no positive lettering or anatomy examples, so recall is unknown.

Omni has fewer sheen disagreements in this corpus, but that is insufficient
evidence to choose a default model. The subjective labels and narrow coverage
need independent review. Quiet anatomy/lettering scores do not establish that
those detectors work.

## Controlled video probe

The same eight-second Veo clip was tested at native 720p, downscaled to 480p,
with its audio removed without re-encoding video, and as the frame at two
seconds at both resolutions. These are one source group. No video accuracy
claim is possible: the clips have not received full temporal ground-truth review.

| Omni input           | Sheen  | Anatomy | Morphing |
| -------------------- | ------ | ------- | -------- |
| 720p video + audio   | 0.3671 | 0.0217  | 0.1290   |
| 480p video + audio   | 0.3595 | 0.0271  | 0.1354   |
| 720p video, silent   | 0.3589 | 0.0243  | 0.1760   |
| 720p frame at 2 s    | 0.3400 | 0.0313  | n/a      |
| 480p frame at 2 s    | 0.3339 | 0.0423  | n/a      |

Resolution changed these scores only slightly in this one example. This does
not explain the original issue's authorship-score difference: the questions,
state, and selected frame differ, and authorship is not measured here. Audio
removal changed the morphing score by 0.047, still below the threshold. Repeat
measurements would be needed to separate representation effects from variability.

Median image-call wall time, including CLI overhead, was 1.20 s for clef,
1.34 s for flash, and 0.90 s for omni on the 22-image base corpus. Calls were
sequential. This is not a controlled model-speed benchmark.

## Next evaluation milestone

1. Human-review the existing provisional labels without looking at model scores.
   Especially resolve the material-sheen and stock-composition rubrics.
2. Collect clear independent positives for garbled lettering, anatomy errors,
   and temporal morphing. Add polished human product photos and generated work
   without defects as difficult negatives. Do not manufacture positive labels
   simply because an asset is generated.
3. Narrow or drop material sheen if reviewers cannot agree on observable criteria.
   Keep stock composition separate from whether an image is good or useful.
4. Review video at its original frame rate, annotate defect timestamps, and test
   whether 2 fps model sampling misses those events. Include ordinary edits and
   intentional animation as negatives.
5. Revise on development data, then freeze questions and thresholds. Collect
   untouched held-out groups for the per-factor gate in the protocol.

No factor has met the launch gate. The result supports another focused evaluation
round; it does not yet support building the public media endpoint.

## Review preparation (2026-10-14)

The 25-sample base pilot now has a local human-review workflow through
`script/review-media`. It presents original images and videos with blank
judgments and the rubric, hiding prior labels, model scores, provenance, and
identifying filenames. Completed reviews can be applied to a separate manifest
without repeating inference. See the review instructions in `README.md`.

No labels have been human-reviewed yet. This follow-up adds no inference calls
or launch evidence; the next steps and recommendation above still apply.
